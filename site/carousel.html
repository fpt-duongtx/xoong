Carousel

<xo-carousel> is a row of slides, its children, that you move through. It is made on the platform: the slides sit in a scroll container with CSS scroll snap, so touch swiping, the wheel and momentum are the browser's own, and the buttons only ask it to scroll. It follows the WAI-ARIA carousel pattern, and a slide that is out of view cannot be reached with Tab. These are the forms it covers, one element each:

Cards, several at a time

items sets how many are visible; set --xo-carousel-items in CSS to change it with the screen width. counter shows the position, and the arrows stop at the ends (or wrap with loop).

A banner or testimonials, moving by itself

One slide at a time with dots and autoplay (milliseconds). It adds a pause button, pauses while the pointer or the focus is inside, does not run under prefers-reduced-motion, and the slides are announced politely only when it is not moving.

A gallery with thumbnails

A slide with data-thumb gives its picture to the button that goes to it, in place of a dot. Keys on the picture area: Left and Right, Home and End.

Slides from data

The slides can come from the data layer: put xo-each on the slide. The carousel follows its children as they arrive and change.

{name}

{email}

{role}
 
<xo-view get="listPeople" p-limit="6">
  <xo-carousel class="service-cards" label="People" items="2" counter no-arrows>
    <article class="card" xo-each="items"><h3>{name}</h3><p>{email}</p><span class="badge">{role}</span></article>
  </xo-carousel>
</xo-view>
APIWhat it does
itemsSlides visible at once (default 1; 1.15 lets the next one peek)
dots, counter, no-arrowsThe position buttons (or thumbnails), "1 / 5", hide the arrows
loop, autoplayNext at the end returns to the first; move on by itself every n milliseconds
index, count, next(), prev(), goTo(i), play(), pause()Control it from code; index is the first visible slide
xo-carousel-changeFires with { index, count }

Not included on purpose: crossfade and 3D styles, an endless loop made of cloned slides, and a continuously scrolling logo strip. They need a different mechanism than scroll snap, and the last two are bad for people who use a keyboard or a screen reader; a CSS animation does the logo strip well.
